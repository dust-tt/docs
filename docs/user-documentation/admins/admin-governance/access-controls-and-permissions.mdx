## **Core concepts definitions**

- **Workspace:** A Dust workspace is the main collaborative environment where teams can interact with AI agents, organize their data through spaces (open or restricted), manage connections to external tools, and control access levels through workspace roles (Admin, Manager, and Member).
- **[Connections](/docs/user-documentation/data-sources/connections):** Connections are fully managed integrations that automatically sync data from external platforms (like Slack, Notion, Google Drive) to Dust, with granular admin control over what specific content is accessible to the workspace
- [**Spaces:**](/docs/user-documentation/admins/spaces-management) Spaces are containers in Dust that let admins organize and control access to data, coming in two types: open spaces accessible to all workspace members and restricted spaces limited to designated users.
- **Users:** A user is a workspace member who can access and interact with Dust agents. Their role and the permissions granted to their groups determine what they can create and manage. See [Workspace governance](/docs/user-documentation/admins/admin-governance/workspace-governance-roles-groups-and-permissions).
- **Admin:** An admin is a workspace user with the highest level of access who can manage all workspace settings, including controlling data connections, managing members' roles, and handling billing. Admins can do everything Managers and Members can do.
- **Manager:** A manager is a delegated workspace administrator who can invite and remove members, change the roles of non-admin members, view Analytics, and configure governance permissions for agents, skills, Frames, and automations.
- **Member:** A member uses Dust according to their seat, group memberships, and the permissions granted to their groups. Members have no administrative access by default.

## Access control policy

- Users with the **Create agents** permission can create agents based on the data of the spaces they have access to.
- Users can interact with agents created with the data of the spaces they are a member of.

## Workspace and space administration

1. **Configure connections**
   1. Authorize Connections to the company platforms and tools (Google Drive, Confluence, GitHub…)
   2. Select data sets scope at “folder” or document level
   3. Monitor data synchronization status
2. **Access policy definition**
   1. Create dedicated spaces (open or restricted)
   2. Segregate company data between open and restricted spaces
   3. Define space membership
3. **Access policy management**
   1. Add or remove spaces data based on user requests
   2. Manage space lifecycle (create, update, delete)
   3. Control members and access permissions
4. **Govern agents fleet**
   1. Deploy company wide agents
   2. Distribute agents across groups
   3. Monitor usage
